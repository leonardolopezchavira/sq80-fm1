A 1987 wavetable synth, in your hands.

Three oscillators. Four envelopes. One filter that sings.

SQ-80 turns the M-VAVE FM-1 into an Ensoniq SQ-80: three wavetable oscillators, a resonant 4-pole filter, four envelopes and three LFOs per voice, eight voices on the FM-1's two cores, with effects, an arpeggiator and a step sequencer. It is measured against the SQ8L emulation to sound like the real instrument.

01 / On the FM-1

Every value, on the FM-1's own screen.

03 / The waves

It plays from the start. It can play yours.

75 waves of its own

SQ-80 comes with a set of 75 waves made from scratch, in the SQ-80's slots and under its names: the plain shapes, the sampled kinds, the drums. No Ensoniq data, so it is ready to play the moment it is installed, with eight banks of programs by Leonardo Lopez Chavira loaded alongside.

Or the originals, from your own ROMs

If you own an SQ-80 or its ROMs, make a ROM pack from your dumps of the four wave ROMs and the OS ROM, in your browser. The installer loads it with your ESQ-1 / SQ-80 SysEx banks, the loader on its own, and the emulator takes it too. Up to eight banks of 40 programs.

The firmware is GPL-3.0-only (the engine MIT), source on GitHub. It's built on fm1-base (MPL-2.0) and felucca-bsp, which come from Felucca by Leo Kuroshita, Hügelton Instruments; its effects are FMini-1's, by Ruppert, after Felucca's; its arpeggiator, sequencer and recording are SLOOP's. The segment lettering is DSEG14 by keshikan (SIL OFL 1.1).
