SQ-80 on the SMK-37

For the M-Vave SMK-37 Pro, the Donner Starrykey 37 Play (the same keyboard), the SMK-37 Elite and the MKE-P37. Tested on a Donner Starrykey 37 Play; the Elite and MKE-P37 builds have not been tried on a keyboard yet.

What you get

The same SQ-80 as on the FM-1, on a keyboard with more to hold on to: 37 keys with velocity, 16 pads, four faders, pitch and mod wheels and a sustain pedal jack.

  • Banks 1–8 are built into the firmware: SQ-80's own eight banks, always there. U1 and U2 are yours: load banks into them, save your programs there.
  • Your ROM pack (the SQ-80's original waves, from your own ROM dumps) goes in over the keyboard's stock presets; without one, SQ-80 plays its own 75 waves.

First: a backup of the keyboard

The keyboard's flash holds things that belong to that one unit: its key and pad calibration and its Bluetooth identity. SQ-80 never writes them, but keep a copy before anything is installed: a full flash backup, read with the chip's own bootloader. It takes about ten minutes, once.

You need a Windows computer (Linux works too, by hand: docs/SMK37-BACKUP.md) and a USB data cable.

1 · Downloads

The package for your keyboard (one of these):

SMK-37 Pro / Starrykey 37 Play SMK-37 Elite MKE-P37

And two folders:

The SQ-80 folder (ZIP) jl-uboot-tool (ZIP)

Unzip both into Downloads; leave the package there as it is. Version {{VERSION}}.

2 · Python 3.12

From python.org: Python 3.12, "Windows installer (64-bit)". On its first screen tick Add python.exe to PATH. (Not 3.13 or newer: the MIDI part has no Windows build for those yet.)

3 · The backup

  1. Connect the keyboard (switched on, M-Vave's firmware running). Close every program that uses MIDI.
  2. In the unzipped SQ-80 folder, double-click smk37-backup.bat and allow it to run as administrator.
  3. Follow it. The keyboard checks the package and restarts in its bootloader; the flash is read twice and the two copies compared. At the end it says backup OK.
  4. Copy the backup folder it made to a USB stick too. Never share those files: they are your unit's.

4 · The install

  1. Switch the keyboard off and on: it comes back in update mode.
  2. Double-click smk37-install.bat. It installs the package and checks it. The keyboard restarts into SQ-80.
  3. Open the Load page in Chrome or Edge: send your ROM pack (the ROM pack maker makes it from your dumps). Banks of your own go to U1 or U2.

Later updates: smk37-install.bat again with the new package (no new backup needed).

The controls

K1Volume
K2 · K3 · K4Bank (1–8, U1, U2) · the section's page · program
K5–K8The four values of the page (on the play screen: cutoff, resonance, attack, release). Where the screen says K1–K4, it means these.
KeysAll 37 play, with their velocity. OCTAVE − / + move them.
PadsThe bank's programs, 16 at a time (< >: the next 16). With SEQ held: the 16 steps.
< >Elsewhere: the section's pages back and forward.
FadersFADER BANK steps A–D (shown on the screen). A: reverb, delay, chorus, drive. B: cutoff, resonance, attack, release. C: the three oscillators' levels and the volume. D: LFO 1 and 2, rate and depth. A fader takes over a value once it reaches it: until then the screen says UP TO or DOWN TO.
Wheels · pedalPitch bend · the SQ-80's WHEEL source · sustain.
ButtonsAs on the FM-1: SCALE = SEL, CHORD = ENV, NOTE REPEAT = LFO, PARA = EDIT, FX, GLOBE = GLO, PATCH = HOME, BT = SAVE, ARP, SEQ, SEQ PLAY = PLAY, SEQ REC = REC.

The rest works as the manual says for the FM-1.

Back to M-Vave's firmware

Install it with M-Vave's updater, as for any update. The calibration and Bluetooth data were never touched. The stock presets SQ-80's ROM pack replaced are in your backup.

The SMK-37 support builds on genkigenki's fm1-to-smk37 kit and SMK-37 layer (GPL-3.0); the backup reads the flash with kagaimiq's jl-uboot-tool.
