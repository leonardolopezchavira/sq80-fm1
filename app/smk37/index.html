SQ-80 on the SMK-37

For the M-Vave SMK-37 Pro, the Donner Starrykey 37 Play (the same keyboard), the SMK-37 Elite and the MKE-P37. Tested on a Donner Starrykey 37 Play; the Elite and MKE-P37 builds have not been tried on a keyboard yet.

What you get

The same SQ-80 as on the FM-1, on a keyboard with more to hold on to: 37 keys with velocity, 16 pads, four faders, pitch and mod wheels and a sustain pedal jack.

  • Banks 1–8 are built into the firmware: SQ-80's own eight banks, always there. U1 and U2 are yours: load banks into them, save your programs there.
  • Your ROM pack (the SQ-80's original waves, from your own ROM dumps) goes in over the keyboard's stock presets; without one, SQ-80 plays its own 75 waves.

Install

Connect the keyboard by USB (switched on, as it is), open the installer for your model in Chrome or Edge and press Install. Nothing to download or set up.

SMK-37 Pro / Starrykey 37 Play SMK-37 Elite MKE-P37

It asks for your ROM pack and up to two banks of your own (they go to U1 and U2), or installs the firmware alone. You can load them later with the Load page; the ROM pack maker makes the pack from your SQ-80's ROM dumps.

Updates: the same installer, the same way. Version {{VERSION}}; the packages: Pro · Elite · MKE-P37.

Optional: a full backup first

SQ-80 never writes the keyboard's own calibration or Bluetooth data, and leaves the stock firmware's data alone except its presets, which the ROM pack replaces. If you want a copy of everything before the first install, read the whole flash on a Windows computer (about ten minutes, once):

  1. Install Python 3.12 from python.org ("Windows installer (64-bit)"; tick Add python.exe to PATH; not 3.13 or newer).
  2. Download and unzip into Downloads: the SQ-80 folder and jl-uboot-tool; and the package for your model (above) into Downloads.
  3. Connect the keyboard, close every program that uses MIDI, and double-click smk37-backup.bat in the SQ-80 folder (allow it as administrator). It ends with backup OK.
  4. Copy the backup folder it made to a USB stick. Never share those files: they are your unit's. Switch the keyboard off and on, then install as above.

Linux, by hand: docs/SMK37-BACKUP.md.

The controls

K1Volume
K2 · K3 · K4Bank (1–8, U1, U2) · the section's page · program
K5–K8The four values of the page (on the play screen: cutoff, resonance, attack, release). Where the screen says K1–K4, it means these.
KeysAll 37 play, with their velocity. OCTAVE − / + move them.
PadsThe bank's programs, 16 at a time (< >: the next 16). With SEQ held: the 16 steps.
< >Elsewhere: the section's pages back and forward.
FadersFADER BANK steps A–D (shown on the screen). A: reverb, delay, chorus, drive. B: cutoff, resonance, attack, release. C: the three oscillators' levels and the volume. D: LFO 1 and 2, rate and depth. A fader takes over a value once it reaches it: until then the screen says UP TO or DOWN TO.
Wheels · pedalPitch bend · the SQ-80's WHEEL source · sustain.
ButtonsAs on the FM-1: SCALE = SEL, CHORD = ENV, NOTE REPEAT = LFO, PARA = EDIT, FX, GLOBE = GLO, PATCH = HOME, BT = SAVE, ARP, SEQ, SEQ PLAY = PLAY, SEQ REC = REC.

The rest works as the manual says for the FM-1.

Back to M-Vave's firmware

Install it with M-Vave's updater, as for any update. The calibration and Bluetooth data were never touched. The stock presets SQ-80's ROM pack replaced are in your backup, if you made one.

The SMK-37 support builds on genkigenki's fm1-to-smk37 kit and SMK-37 layer (GPL-3.0); the optional backup reads the flash with kagaimiq's jl-uboot-tool.
